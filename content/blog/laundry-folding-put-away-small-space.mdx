A load is not done when it is dry. It is done when it has a home. In a small house the dry pile often becomes a second sofa cushion, then it migrates back into the dirty basket.

This guide is the last step of the [small-space laundry system](/blog/small-space-laundry-system): a folding station, a clean-clothes container, and a put-away path. Collection belongs in the [hamper guide](/blog/family-laundry-hamper-system). Drying belongs in the [rack guide](/blog/indoor-clothes-drying-rack-small-space).

## Short answer

Fold where you can sit, on a surface you already have, with a container that is only for clean clothes.

1. Empty the rack or dryer onto a table, bed, or counter—not onto the dirty hamper.
2. Sit if standing to fold is a balance problem or a back problem.
3. Make one stack per person or per drawer, not one perfect cube.
4. Put away the stacks before the next wash starts.
5. Let children carry only their own small stack, in a basket with a listed weight they can manage.

A bed is a legal folding table. So is the dining table after supper. Do not buy a folding-board cabinet unless the house has nowhere to set a shirt down.

## Why clean piles fail

They fail when clean and dirty share a basket. They fail when the only fold surface is also dinner. They fail when putting away means a high closet only an adult can reach.

Keep three landings separate:

- **Dirty** where clothes come off
- **In progress** on the rack or in the machine
- **Clean** in a basket or stack that never sits in the dirty container

If you have only one basket, dump it empty before it becomes the clean-clothes bin. Wipe it if it held damp towels.

## A usable station

**Surface.** Clear one end of a table. A hard surface folds faster than a bedspread, but a bed is better than the floor.

**Seat.** A dining chair, a stool, or the edge of the bed. Hopping on one foot to match socks is how the pile returns.

**Light.** You need to see stains you are going to rewash. Overhead light is enough. This is not a craft station.

**Time.** Fold one load before starting the next. A “later” pile in a small room becomes furniture.

Hanging replaces folding for shirts that you already hang. One hook on a door is a put-away point. It does not need a special hanger tree.

## Children

Give a child their stack and their drawer, not the whole family’s laundry.

- Their clean basket lives at their height.
- Socks can be a paired pile, not a retail fold.
- Towels and adult work clothes stay on the adult stack.
- “Put your pile away” is the sentence. Inspecting the fold is optional.

If a child cannot reach the drawer, the clothes will land on the chair. Lower the drawer or use a low cubby. The [hamper guide](/blog/family-laundry-hamper-system) is the dirty-clothes version of the same rule.

Skip it if you need more than 9 pounds, or if you already have a small tote that can be the clean-only basket. A paper grocery bag works for one put-away trip.

Do not use this as the dirty hamper and the clean hamper on the same afternoon without emptying it.

## Who should skip a formal station

Skip extra furniture if the dining table already works. Skip a dedicated laundry room fold counter if that room is also the only hall.

If folding while standing is painful or unsafe, sit. If putting away a full load is too much, put away one person’s stack and leave the rest in a *clean* basket, labeled by being in a different room from the dirty one.

## Maintenance

Once a week, look at the chair that collects clothes. If it is never empty, the put-away step is missing—not the detergent. Send off-season clothes out of the rotation so the drawers can accept a Monday load.
