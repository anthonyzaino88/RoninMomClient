A hamper fails when it is in the wrong room, too heavy to carry, or so large that wash day becomes a mountain. The container is not the habit. The path is.

This guide is for choosing a collection format: open carry basket, small bedroom basket, or a narrow standing sorter. The [laundry-system guide](/blog/small-space-laundry-system) covers the whole path. Wet clothes coming out of the washer still need air—see [indoor drying](/blog/indoor-clothes-drying-rack-small-space).

## Short answer

Put a collection point where clothes come off. Size it to one washable load, not to the whole house.

- **Open carry basket** when you need to move laundry to a machine or a shared laundry room and you can live with seeing the clothes.
- **Small bedroom basket** when each person (or each child) has a reachable container with a listed weight limit.
- **Narrow standing sorter** when the spare slice of floor is a few inches wide and you want whites and colors split before wash day.

A pillowcase, a tote, or a cardboard box can stand in for any of these. A handmade willow basket or a thrifted crate can fill the open-basket or standing-sorter job if the size you measure fits. The format only has to be easier than the floor.

## Decision criteria

1. **Where clothes come off.** Bedroom, bath, and mud-adjacent hall are different landings.
2. **Carry.** Stairs, a laundromat, or a long hall favor a bag with a handle you can hold while opening a door.
3. **Weight.** Use the listed maximum load when the manufacturer publishes one. Do not fill past what the carrier can lift.
4. **Footprint.** Measure width and depth with the door open.
5. **Sorting.** Two compartments help only if someone will actually use them. One basket per person is another way to sort.
6. **Moisture.** Damp towels in a closed bag sit wet. An open basket dries faster and looks messier.
7. **Child reach.** If a child cannot put clothes in without help, the floor next to the hamper becomes the hamper.

## Open basket versus standing bag

**Open flexible basket.** Helps for carrying and for wet clothes leaving the washer. Costs you a visible pile and no lid.

**Small mesh or flexible basket.** Helps in a wardrobe or child’s room. Costs you capacity. One researched 6-gallon basket lists a 9-pound maximum load.

**Narrow standing bag on a frame.** Helps in a slice of bathroom or closet. Costs you assembly, a depth measurement, and a smaller total volume.

**Large collapsible tote.** Helps for laundromat runs. Costs you bulk when it is full, and it may slump when empty unless the page says it stands.

Do not use a lidded bin as the only home for wet towels. They need air, same as shoes on a closed cabinet.

## Children and carry weight

A child-sized basket is useful when it holds *their* clothes and an adult still carries the family tote.

Keep the instruction ordinary: clothes go in the basket, not beside it. Overflow is an adult reset, not a lecture.

If someone has limited grip, balance, or lifting, prefer a bag they can slide, a low open basket they do not have to hoist, or doing laundry in smaller, more frequent loads. A rolling bin can help some households; it also needs floor that wheels can actually use.

## Willow and a thrifted basket

A handmade willow hamper can do the same job as a plastic carry basket if the published size fits the doorway and the load limit matches what you actually carry. Willow is not a plastic tub. Use the listed care: wipe the weave dry.

<ProductLink productId="ikea-tolkning-laundry-basket-11" placement="hamper-willow-carry">IKEA TOLKNING willow carry basket</ProductLink>

TOLKNING is a handmade willow laundry basket with a removable polyester bag. IKEA lists it at 22 × 15 × 11 inches, 11 gallons, and a 13-pound maximum load. The page also notes polypropylene feet and says you can keep the basket in a bathroom if you avoid soaking it and wipe it dry. Use it as a low carry basket, not as a standing sorter and not as a place to store soaking-wet towels.

Skip it if 13 pounds is less than you carry, or if you need a lid. A tote you already own is the substitute.

<ProductLink productId="ikea-nattgibba-laundry-basket-26" placement="hamper-willow-sorter">IKEA NATTGIBBA willow sorter</ProductLink>

NATTGIBBA is a taller handmade willow basket on a powder-coated steel frame. IKEA lists 23 ½ × 14 ¼ × 24 inches, 26 gallons, and a 33-pound maximum load. The page describes two compartments for sorting and a removable inner bag. Assembly is required; it ships flat. That is a standing sorter with a woven exterior, not a second TORKIS.

Skip it if you cannot assemble the frame, cannot spare 23 ½ by 14 ¼ inches, or need a low carry tote. Two pillowcases still sort without a stand.

A thrifted woven hamper or wooden crate can fill either job if you check it in person. Measure the footprint against the landing spot. Lift it with a realistic load and see whether the handles or rim stay attached. Skip cracked wood, a collapsing weave, or a basket that already smells musty. A lidded hamper is a poor only home for wet towels; leave the lid off or use an open basket for that load.

## Maintenance

Empty on a schedule you can keep, not when the hamper is a hazard. Wipe a plastic basket. Wipe willow dry; do not soak it. Wash a removable fabric bag as its own care line says—PURRPINGLA is hand wash; the TOLKNING and NATTGIBBA inner bags list a machine-wash-warm cycle. Do not store damp towels in a closed sorter overnight if you can hang them.

If the landing is full again, the hamper is not too small. Too many clothes are living in the wrong room. Send extras to the dresser they belong in.
