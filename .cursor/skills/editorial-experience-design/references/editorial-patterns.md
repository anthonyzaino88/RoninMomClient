# Editorial Patterns

Choose the smallest set of modules that creates a clear reading path. Do not treat this as a checklist to place on every page.

## Homepage

1. **Promise and path:** A specific value proposition, authentic image or editorial composition, and one primary route into the journal.
2. **Editorial lead:** One timely or foundational story with enough context to explain why it matters.
3. **Pillar entry points:** The three Ronin Mom pillars, differentiated through useful framing rather than identical icon cards.
4. **Useful sequence:** A compact mix of recent, practical, and evergreen reading. Curate; do not merely dump the newest posts.
5. **Relationship step:** A newsletter invitation with a concrete benefit, frequency only if confirmed, and an honest privacy expectation.

## Journal and category pages

- Open with a short explanation of who the collection helps and what questions it answers.
- Support discovery with a clear featured story, scannable article list, and filters only when the content volume justifies them.
- Expose title, useful description, category, date when meaningful, and consistent image treatment.
- Handle small collections gracefully; do not fabricate categories or pad the page.

## Article pages

- Keep breadcrumbs and metadata secondary to the title and reader promise.
- Use an introduction that confirms the problem and outcome before optional personal context.
- Target a comfortable reading measure of roughly 60–75 characters per line for body copy.
- Make headings, lists, callouts, figures, sources, and related links visually distinct without turning the article into a dashboard.
- Use a table of contents only for genuinely long or multi-part guides.
- End with a practical next step and one or two closely related articles, not an endless recommendation grid.

## Editorial components

Use components for repeated meaning, not merely repeated decoration. Useful candidates include:

- Article dek or summary
- Tested-note or personal-experience callout, only when truthful
- Safety or evidence note
- Step sequence
- Materials or resource list
- Comparison table
- Source list
- Related-reading group
- Newsletter invitation

Each component must preserve semantic HTML, work without hover, support keyboard use where interactive, and remain readable at 320px width and 200% zoom.

## Visual restraint

- Let type scale, whitespace, rules, image crops, captions, and selective color create hierarchy.
- Use one dominant and one supporting action per section at most.
- Reserve accents for navigation, status, or emphasis with a consistent meaning.
- Avoid identical section rhythms; alternate image-led, text-led, and list-led modules only when the content supports them.
